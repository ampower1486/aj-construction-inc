5. The construction contract governs

Your project is governed by the signed written contract between you and AJ Construction Inc, not by this website. That contract sets out the scope of work, the materials, the price, the payment schedule, the approximate start and completion dates, the change order process and the warranty. Where anything on this website differs from that contract, the contract controls.

California law requires home improvement contracts to be in writing and to contain specific terms and notices. We will not begin work on a home improvement project without that signed written contract in place.

6. Down payments and progress payments

For home improvement contracts, California law limits the down payment to $1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less, excluding finance charges. We comply with that limit. Any contractor asking a homeowner for more than that as a down payment on a home improvement contract is acting outside the law.

Payments after the down payment are tied to work actually completed or materials actually delivered, as set out in the payment schedule in your contract. We do not ask you to pay ahead of the work.

7. Your right to cancel

For contracts signed at your home or anywhere other than our place of business, you have the right to cancel within three business days of signing. If you are 65 years of age or older, that period is five business days. Your contract includes a written notice of cancellation form explaining exactly how to do it.

Separate and additional cancellation rights apply to contracts to repair or restore damage from a declared disaster. If that applies to your situation, we will tell you and the notice will be in your contract.

8. Permits and inspections

Where a permit is required, your contract states who obtains it. As a licensed contractor we will not perform permit-required work without the permit, and we will not ask you to pull an owner-builder permit so that the work can avoid our licence and our responsibility.

Inspection timing is set by the local building department and is outside our control. Delays caused by inspection scheduling, plan review or utility connections are not treated as our default.

9. Mechanics lien notice

Under California law, contractors, subcontractors, labourers and material suppliers who are not paid for work or materials supplied to your property have the right to record a mechanics lien against it. This applies to any construction project, with any contractor.

You may receive a Preliminary Notice from suppliers or sub-trades on your job. That is a routine legal step, not an accusation that anything is wrong. To protect yourself, you are entitled to ask us for unconditional lien releases from anyone who has sent you a preliminary notice before you make each payment, and we will provide them.
